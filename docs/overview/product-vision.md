# Product Vision

Pundit's premise: the most useful thing you can do with a prediction model isn't stare at a probability table, it's ask it a question and get a straight answer.

Most football analytics tools stop at a dashboard — win probabilities, expected goals, a standings table. Pundit puts a conversational layer on top: name a matchup, ask about the title race, or query the league table, and Pundit tells you what the numbers say, where the model and the market disagree, and what would have to be true for an upset — grounded in a real statistical model and live market pricing rather than an LLM guessing.

**Today**, that's an analysis desk: a live fixture slate, an analyst pane on `POST /api/ask`, and a local paper lab (Paper / Draft / Vaults). Fundamental v3 preserves its rating/home-advantage 1X2 probabilities and uses reviewed historical EPL results to calibrate scoring rates and low-score shape. Totals, BTTS, scorelines and calibrated expected goals come from the final joint score grid, which also supports season and paper simulations. UCL qualifiers, neutral and pre-origin fixtures retain the baseline 2.70-total shape. ESPN standings, a complete-schedule-gated Premier League season simulator and reference Stake/Kalshi/Polymarket prices surround that model. Production loads pinned artifacts locally. Historical improvement is retrospective evidence: the cohorts were exposed during development, and the secondary totals interval includes zero. Future superiority remains unproven. The rolling club-season ledger and frozen World Cup backtest preserve their own evidence boundaries.

**Where this is designed to grow:** the same grounded-analysis approach extends naturally to additional competitions, richer market coverage, and deeper calibration — without reintroducing trading or onchain mechanics. Paper screens are a simulator, not a shipped book. This section describes direction, not a financial commitment.
